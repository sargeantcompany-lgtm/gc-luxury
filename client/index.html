Marketing Is the Baseline, Not the Edge

When everyone's marketing is excellent, excellent marketing alone no longer sets a property apart.

If you have been on the market, or watched it closely of late, you will have seen properties sit unsold through one agent, then a second, with marketing and presentation that barely changes. Many homes share similar architecture and end up looking the same online, and the marketing offers little to tell them apart. One agent's video might be slightly better, another's drone photography sharper, but nothing truly stands out. The standard of marketing across the Gold Coast is outstanding, and it's hard to fault the industry's effort. When everyone's marketing is excellent, though, excellent marketing alone no longer sets a property apart.

Agent marketing is an important part of selling. But it is only the baseline of what's needed to sell a property at the highest possible price.

The basics every agent should provide

  • Marketing video
  • Floorplans, drone photography and professional photography
  • Home staging, where appropriate
  • Premium listings on the major portals (realestate.com.au and domain.com.au)
  • Admin preparation, including AML, disclosure and brochures

Beyond the basics are the buyer database and social media.

A qualified buyer is different.

They are searching specifically for the right property, and for them, value isn't about the lowest price. It's about paying a fair price for the right home. An agent will typically hold just five to ten of these buyers at any time.

Beyond that, most buyers on a database have no loyalty built on a trusted relationship. If they see a property online, they will attend the open home and engage with whichever agent has the listing.

Social Media

Reach versus results

Social media is a very different space in today's property market, so it's essential to understand how it really works when promoting your home.

The basics are geographic targeting with an eye-catching video or set of photos, usually through the Domain or realestate.com.au social media push. These campaigns benefit the portals as much as they benefit the agent and your property. Creative video has real merit, and a great one will catch attention. What matters most, though, is who is watching. In most cases, it's the people who live around your property, not the buyers you actually need. Beyond that, some agents use a slightly different approach, reaching people who visit the area and search real estate-related keywords.

Almost all of this activity happens on Meta, meaning Facebook and Instagram. A few companies have dedicated professionals who can reach buyers interstate and overseas. That takes experience, and usually an agency with longevity in real estate social media. Even then, the question remains: how many buyers actually come from social media?

If you and your friends have seen your property online, it feels like the campaign is working. But is it? People often click and fill in enquiry forms out of boredom, and those enquiries lead nowhere.
