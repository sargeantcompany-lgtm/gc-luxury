Step One

Your Sale Strategy

Everything starts here. Your strategy decides your price range, your method of sale, your marketing and your timeline — every step below is built on it.

The Appraisal

A detailed assessment of what your home is worth in today's market.

Read more

We start by inspecting your property in person. We look at its land, position, water frontage, outlook, condition and finishes — the details that set it apart. We then compare it with recent sales and current listings nearby, and with the buyer demand we're seeing right now. You receive a clear, written appraisal that explains how we reached our figures.

Your Appraisal Range

Why we give you a range, not a single number.

Read more

No two buyers value a home the same way, and prestige and waterfront homes are rarely like-for-like with other sales. A range reflects that reality. The lower end is what the market evidence clearly supports. The upper end is what the right buyer, in a competitive campaign, could pay. Where your home lands within that range depends on presentation, marketing and how well the campaign is negotiated — which is exactly what the rest of this process is designed to deliver.

Your Campaign Schedule

Every booking, in order, so you know exactly what happens and when.

Once the strategy is set, we book everything in. A typical four-week campaign looks like this:

  1. Week 0

    Strategy meeting and appraisal. Sign the Form 6. Order the disclosure statement, AML check and pool safety certificate.

  2. Preparation week

    Styling and any small repairs. Photography, drone, floor plan and video. Copywriting and design.

  3. Week 1

    Launch: Domain, realestate.com.au, the Ray White network, buyer database, signboard and flyers.

  4. Weeks 1–4

    Open homes and private inspections. Weekly buyer feedback and campaign reports.

  5. Week 4

    Auction day or offers deadline. Contract signed.

  6. Settlement

    Usually 30–60 days after the contract is signed. Keys handed over.

The schedule is tailored to your home and method of sale. An off-market campaign may be shorter, and an expressions of interest campaign may run longer.

Step Two

Appointing Your Agent: The Form 6 Agreement

The agreement that formally appoints us to sell your home.

PriceNo cost to sign — commission and marketing are set out inside it.

Read more

In Queensland, an agent can't start work until you've both signed a Form 6 (Appointment of a Property Agent). It's the contract between you and your agent. It sets out exactly what we'll do, what it costs and how long the appointment runs. Nothing is hidden — every fee is written down before we begin.

What's in your Form 6:

  • Type of appointment — who can earn commission if the property sells:
    • Exclusive agency — we are your only agent and earn commission however the sale happens, including if you find the buyer yourself.
    • Sole agency — we are your only agent, but no commission is payable if you sell privately to a buyer you found.
    • Open listing — you can appoint several agents; only the agent who effectively causes the sale is paid.
  • Term — for a residential sale, a sole or exclusive appointment can run for up to 90 days. It can then continue as an open listing if you agree.
  • Ending the appointment — how and when either party can end it, with the notice required, is written into the form.
  • Price — your asking price or the method of sale (for example, auction).
  • Commission — the agreed amount or percentage, including GST, and when it's payable (normally at settlement).
  • Marketing and expenses — your itemised marketing budget and any other costs you authorise us to spend.
  • Rebates and benefits — any rebate, discount or referral benefit we receive from a service provider must be disclosed.
  • Special conditions — anything specific to your sale, such as excluded buyers.

We'll walk through every clause with you before you sign, and you'll receive a signed copy to keep.

Step Three

Method of Sale

There are only a few ways to sell a home. We'll recommend the one that suits your property, your timing and the market.

Private Treaty

A clear asking price, with offers negotiated privately.

PriceNo additional cost

Read more

Your home is marketed with a price, and buyers make offers that we negotiate on your behalf. Private treaty suits sellers who want a clear price, where timing isn't critical, or where marketing budgets are smaller. Pricing is the key: buyers often spend 10–15% more than they planned, so we price to bring them through the door, not lose them before they inspect. In Queensland, a residential buyer under a private treaty contract usually has a 5 business day cooling-off period.

Auction

A set date, competitive bidding and no price ceiling.

PriceAuctioneer fee (see Marketing)

Read more

Auction is a focused campaign that brings the maximum number of buyers together at one time. A set date makes buyers act, competitive bidding removes the price barrier, and if your reserve is met the sale is unconditional under the hammer, with no cooling-off period. You set the reserve, so your position is protected. Under Queensland law, agents can't advertise a price for an auction property, which keeps the market open to every buyer. Auction suits sellers who want to shorten the time to sale, attract every possible buyer and create urgency.

Expressions of Interest / Tender

Buyers submit their best offer by a set closing date.

PriceNo additional cost

Read more

Buyers lodge written offers by a closing date, without seeing what others have offered. It works well for unique or prestige properties that are hard to price, and lets you compare offers on price and terms side by side.

Off-Market

A discreet sale to qualified buyers, without public advertising.

PriceNo additional cost

Read more

Your property is presented privately to our database of qualified buyers and our network, with no portal listing or signboard. It suits sellers who value privacy, or who want to test the market before a public campaign.

Section 1

Government & Compliance

Legal requirements every Queensland seller must meet before a buyer signs.

Seller Disclosure Statement

Your legal disclosure package, prepared before you go to market.

Price$990

Read more

Queensland's seller disclosure laws require every seller to give buyers a signed Disclosure Statement, plus the prescribed certificates, before the buyer signs a contract. It covers title details, the registered plan, rates and water notices, zoning and environmental information, body corporate details where they apply, and pool compliance. If it's missing or incorrect, the buyer may be able to terminate the contract. Having it prepared professionally at the start means your campaign launches compliant and your contracts hold up.

Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Verification

Identity and compliance checks required under federal law.

Price$180

Read more

Under Australia's Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing laws, real estate agents have formal obligations to verify who they are acting for, confirm the beneficial owners where a property is held in a company or trust, and keep records. This check is completed before your property goes to market. It protects you, the buyer and the transaction.

Pool Safety Certificate

Required if your property has a pool or spa.

Read more

In Queensland, a property with a regulated pool or spa needs a current Pool Safety Certificate, issued by a licensed pool safety inspector, for the sale. If one isn't in place, a Notice of No Pool Safety Certificate (Form 36) must be given to the buyer before the contract is signed, and the buyer must obtain a certificate after settlement. Arranging the inspection early means any issues are fixed before buyers can use them to negotiate on price.
