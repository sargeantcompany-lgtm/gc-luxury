What the research says about homes that haven't sold

  • Time on the market works against a property. Once a home has been listed for a while, it starts chasing the market instead of leading it. Buyers have already seen it, the portals push it down the list, and newer listings take the attention.
  • Day 45 is the turning point. Our data shows that after 45 days, a second agent has a better chance of selling a property than the first. It comes down to a fresh launch and new buyers in front of the home.
  • Auction sells faster. Auction properties average 26 days on market, compared with 54 for private treaty, and there's an 83% chance of selling within 45 days of listing.
  • Expired or withdrawn doesn't mean unsellable. A relaunch with a new campaign, a new buyer pool and a firm date gives a home a second chance to be seen as new.

That's why I'd like to invite you to retest the market at our Summer Auction Experience on Saturday 30 January 2027 at The Star Gold Coast, held in the room and online.

Why this could be the best window to sell for some time

Several things are lining up this summer that may not come together again for a while:

  • Peak buyer activity online. realestate.com.au recorded 216 million visits to its Buy section last summer, up 13% on the year before, and buyer enquiries rose 14%. Buyers are on holidays with time to scroll, inspect and decide.
  • More inspections through the door. Across our five offices, we're forecasting more than 2,000 January inspections on the Gold Coast, while many agents write January off. With that many people through our open homes, chances are the buyer for your property is already walking through one of our doors.
  • A gap in interest rate rises. The Reserve Bank has lifted rates five times this year. Economists now expect a pause in December, but February could still bring another rise, and rate cuts aren't expected until late 2027. A sale at the end of January lands inside this gap, before buyers' borrowing power could tighten again.
  • Buyers have a reason to act. Many want to be settled before the school year, and an auction date gives them a firm deadline.

Bringing back the fear of missing out

What drove the market for the last six years was the fear of missing out. Buyers knew that if they hesitated, someone else would buy the home. Since then, many buyers have swung the other way and believe that if they wait, they'll do better.

An auction brings that urgency back. Buyers know there's one date, one room and one chance. They're bidding against each other in real time, and the only way to secure the home is to act on the day. The same fear of missing out that drove the last six years could be exactly what gets your property sold in January.
